Has the 12-Team Playoff Changed Non-Conference Scheduling?

DTSC 2301 — Portfolio Project 1 · Micah Shelley

Research Question

Has the expansion of the College Football Playoff from 4 to 12 teams changed how Power Conference teams schedule non-conference opponents? Specifically, did the average non-conference opponent win percentage drop in the 2024–2025 season compared to the four-team era (2014–2023)?

Method

Using game and season-record data from the College Football Data API, I collected every non-conference game played by a Power 4 team (ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC) from 2014 through 2025, and looked up each opponent's overall win percentage for that season. I then compared the average opponent win percentage between the four-team playoff era (2014–2023) and the twelve-team era (2024–2025), and tested the difference with a two-sample t-test.

Conclusion: No, the data does not support the idea that non-conference opponents got easier after the Playoff expanded to 12 teams. If anything, the average opponent win percentage went up very slightly (50.1% → 50.8%) in the new era, not down — but that tiny difference isn't statistically significant (p = 0.55), so the honest conclusion is that scheduling behavior hasn't meaningfully changed, at least not yet, with only two seasons of data in the new format.
